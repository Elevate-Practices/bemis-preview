Common dental emergencies we treat

If any of these are happening to you or your child, call us:

  • Severe toothache and pain
  • Abscess, swelling or infection
  • A broken or cracked tooth
  • A lost filling, crown or bridge
  • A broken or lost denture or night guard
  • Throbbing pain that won’t go away

A dental emergency can get worse quickly. If you have a fever, swelling in your face or jaw, or pus around a tooth, you may have an infection and should be seen right away.

Questions people ask when they’re hurting

“I don’t have insurance.”

Come in anyway. Our membership plan includes an emergency exam each year and saves 20% on treatment, with no waiting period. CareCredit lets you pay monthly.

“I’m afraid it will hurt more.”

We numb the area before treatment, and nitrous oxide or oral sedation is available if you’re nervous.

“Is it really an emergency?”

If it hurts, is swollen, or something broke, call us. We’ll help you decide what to do and how soon to come in.

“Should I go to the ER instead?”

For a toothache or broken tooth, a dentist can usually treat the cause. If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, or swelling that’s spreading fast, call 911 or go to the ER.

Dental emergency questions

How soon can I be seen?

We offer same-day or next-day appointments for patients who need immediate treatment, during office hours. Call (817) 237-9701 and tell us what happened.

What should I do for tooth pain until I’m seen?

An over-the-counter anti-inflammatory like ibuprofen can help, along with a cold compress on the outside of your cheek for 20 minutes at a time. If you have a fever or facial swelling, call us right away.

Do you see children for emergencies?

Yes. We treat dental emergencies for patients of all ages.
