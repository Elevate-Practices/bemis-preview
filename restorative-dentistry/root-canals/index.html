What is a Root Canal?

A root canal is a procedure designed to treat infection inside the tooth’s pulp (the soft tissue containing nerves and blood vessels). When left untreated, this infection can cause severe pain, abscesses, and even tooth loss. A root canal removes the infected tissue, disinfects the inside of the tooth, and seals it to prevent future issues.

Signs You May Need a Root Canal

  • Severe tooth pain that lingers, especially when biting or chewing
  • Increased sensitivity to hot or cold temperatures
  • Swollen or tender gums near the affected tooth
  • A pimple-like bump on the gums (sign of an abscess)
  • Tooth discoloration or darkening of the enamel

If you are experiencing any of these symptoms, it’s important to schedule an evaluation as soon as possible to prevent further complications.

The Root Canal Procedure

1. Examination and Diagnosis

Dr. Bemis will examine your tooth, take digital X-rays, and determine if a root canal is the best treatment for your condition. If an infection is confirmed, we will explain the procedure in detail and ensure you feel comfortable before starting treatment.

2. Removing the Infection

After numbing the area with local anesthesia, a small opening is made in the tooth. The infected pulp is carefully removed, and the inner chamber is thoroughly cleaned and disinfected to eliminate bacteria.

3. Sealing and Restoring the Tooth

Once the infection is cleared, the tooth is sealed with a biocompatible material to prevent reinfection. In many cases, a dental crown is placed over the treated tooth to restore its strength and function.

Benefits of Root Canal Treatment

  • Eliminates tooth pain and discomfort
  • Prevents the spread of infection to surrounding teeth and gums
  • Preserves your natural tooth instead of requiring an extraction
  • Restores full function so you can eat and speak normally
  • Improves oral health by preventing future complications

Does a Root Canal Hurt?

Modern root canal therapy is virtually painless thanks to advanced dental techniques and anesthesia. Most patients compare the procedure to getting a routine dental filling. After treatment, mild soreness is normal, but it can be managed with over-the-counter pain relievers.

Questions

Root Canals: common questions

How long does a root canal take?

Most root canals can be completed in one to two visits, depending on the severity of the infection.

Will I need a crown after my root canal?

In most cases, yes. Since a root canal-treated tooth is more fragile, a dental crown helps restore its strength and longevity.

How long will my treated tooth last?

With proper care, a root canal-treated tooth can last a lifetime. Regular brushing, flossing, and dental checkups are essential for maintaining oral health.

Can I eat after my root canal?

It’s best to wait until the numbness wears off before eating. Stick to soft foods for the first day and avoid chewing on the treated side until fully healed.
